\begin{questions}
  
  \question 
    When a spaceship is orbiting the earth and wants to land, it fires \emph{retro thrusters}, which are rockets that fire in the opposite direction than the spaceship is moving.  This causes the spaceship to slow down.  Explain why slowing down will cause the ship to come in for a landing. \vs

  \question
    If the Sun and its gravity were to suddenly disappear, what would happen to the motion of the earth? \vs

  \question A certain astronaut has a mass of 92~kg.

  \begin{parts}
    \part Find the force of gravity acting on the astronaut when she is on the earth. \vs

    \part The International Space Station (ISS) orbits at a radius of \SI{6.78e6}{m} from the center of the Earth.  Calculate the force of gravity acting on the astronaut when she is aboard the ISS. \vs

    \part It should be clear from these calculations that even though the astronaut has a moderately lower force of gravity, it is nowhere near zero gravity.  Why, then, does the astronaut experience ``weightlessness''? \vspace{5em}
  \end{parts}
